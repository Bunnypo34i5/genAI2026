### Opening

Dear Sir/Madam,

We are students at Hong Kong Baptist University undertaking a policy research project examining the implementation of the Electronic Health Record Sharing System (Amendment) Ordinance 2025, which came into effect on 1 December 2025.

We wish to submit a formal enquiry **under the Code on Access to Information** regarding the enforcement mechanisms, data definitions, and current implementation status of the amended legislation.

---

### SECTION A: ENFORCEMENT AND COMPLIANCE

**1. Enforcement Mechanism**
   - Is there a routine audit or inspection system monitoring eHealth data-deposit compliance, or does the government only investigate upon receiving a complaint?
   - How many inspectors or compliance officers are currently assigned to monitor eHealth data-deposit obligations?

**2. Definition of "Repeatedly Ignoring" Data-Input Orders**
   - What constitutes "repeatedly" ignoring a data-input order that triggers the HK$50,000 fine?
   - How many instances of non-compliance are required before a fine is imposed?
   - Is there a formal warning process and timeline before penalties are enforced?

**3. Enforcement Actions Since 1 December 2025**
   - How many healthcare providers have been issued a data-deposit order by the Secretary for Health since the amendment took effect?
   - How many warnings or penalties have been issued for non-compliance with mandatory data-deposit obligations?
   - If no enforcement actions have been taken, what is the expected timeline?

---

### SECTION B: DATA DEFINITION AND SCOPE

**4. Specified Health Data Categories**
   - What is the precise list of "specified health data" that healthcare providers are currently required to deposit?
   - Has the Secretary for Health formally designated specific data types and healthcare professional categories subject to mandatory deposit?
   - If not yet designated, what is the expected timeline?

**5. Criteria for "Specified" Healthcare Providers**
   - How does the government determine which healthcare providers are "specified" and subject to mandatory data-deposit obligations?
   - What criteria or thresholds are used (e.g., practice size, patient volume, medical specialty)?

---

### SECTION C: PATIENT RIGHTS AND PRIVACY

**6. Patient Opt-Out Mechanisms**
   - How can a patient exercise the right to request that their healthcare provider does not deposit their data to eHealth, even after the provider joins the system?
   - Is there a formal opt-out mechanism (e.g., a standardised form)?
   - How is this patient right communicated to the public?

**7. Data Security and Unauthorised Access Prevention**
   - What safeguards are in place to prevent unauthorised access to patient data by healthcare providers, particularly cross-boundary providers?
   - How does the government verify and continuously monitor "recognised" healthcare professionals outside Hong Kong?

---

### SECTION D: ADOPTION AND INCENTIVES

**8. eHealth+ Connectivity Support Scheme Uptake (October 2025–Present)**
   - How many private healthcare providers have applied for the eHealth+ Connectivity Support Scheme since its launch?
   - What is the approval rate?
   - What percentage of eligible providers have submitted applications?

**9. eHealth+ Connectivity Accreditation Scheme**
   - What is the current uptake of the eHealth+ Connectivity Accreditation Scheme?
   - How many providers have achieved gold, silver, or bronze accreditation?

**10. Private-Sector Data Deposit Rates**
   - Since the amendment took effect (1 December 2025), what has been the change in private-sector healthcare data-deposit rates?
   - Can the government provide monthly statistics comparing the period before and after 1 December 2025?
   - What is the government's target for private-sector data-deposit rates by the end of 2026, and what key performance indicators are being used to measure success?

---

### SECTION E: SYSTEM EFFECTIVENESS AND INTEGRATION

**11. Insurance Reimbursement and eHealth Integration**
   - Has the Health Bureau considered extending eHealth functionality to support automated insurance reimbursement (e.g., Voluntary Health Insurance Scheme (VHIS) claims processing)?
   - If yes, what is the current progress?
   - If not, what technical, regulatory, or policy barriers have been identified?

**12. Feasibility Study on Insurance Integration**
   - Has any feasibility study been conducted on integrating eHealth with the private insurance industry?
   - What is the current number of eHealth users with linked insurance verification data?

---

### SECTION F: CROSS-BOUNDARY HEALTHCARE SERVICES

**13. Recognised Healthcare Providers Outside Hong Kong**
   - How many healthcare providers outside Hong Kong have been "recognised" under the new cross-boundary provisions?
   - Which jurisdictions are currently covered?
   - How is patient data security maintained for cross-boundary records?

---

### SECTION G: FOLLOW-UP FROM 2020 SCMP LETTER

**14. Registration Simplification**
   - Has the government simplified the registration process for private healthcare professionals since 2020?
   - Is online registration now available (as called for in the 2020 SCMP letter)?
   - Is registration now required for all providers in the Health Care Voucher Scheme?

**15. eHealth Access Statistics and Usage**
   - What are the current eHealth access statistics for 2025/2026?
   - How do these figures compare with 2019 baseline data (656,140 accesses, approximately 32 per doctor)?
   - Has the amendment measurably increased usage among private healthcare professionals?

## ASSESSMENT OF QUESTION SUBSTANTIVE VALUE

✅ **Substantive Questions:**

1. **Enforcement gaps** — The questions expose whether the government has implemented monitoring (A1–A3). This is critical because statutory obligations without enforcement lose credibility.

2. **Private-sector adoption crisis** — Questions B8–B10 directly address Hong Kong's persistent problem: private healthcare data remains <1% of the system despite government targets. This is empirically documented (SCMP 2024; LCQ 2025).

3. **Patient consent ambiguity** — Question C6 identifies a gap: patients' right to opt out is stated in law but unclear how to exercise it. This is a real implementation problem.

4. **Insurance +eHealth nexus** — Questions E11–E12 pivot the policy discussion from "data for data's sake" to "data for service delivery," which aligns with government rhetoric on "One Health Data Repository" but has no documented insurance use case.

5. **Cross-boundary data security** — Question F13 is timely given early-stage discussions on mainland cooperation (per eHealth+ 2025 plan).
